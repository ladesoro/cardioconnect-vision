Why the MVP matters

The current MVP creates the foundation for what comes next.

CardioConnect’s first release gives BSC a shared scheduling and assignment layer. The future vision extends that foundation across the moments before, during, and after each case.

Shared work intakeA more consistent way to create procedure, non-procedure, and unavailable-time work.
Assignment visibilityTeams can see coverage status and reassign work with more confidence.
Mobile field accessReps and leaders can view the day’s work without relying only on desktop tools.
Operating model clarityThe MVP exposes the workflow patterns future phases can connect and optimize.

Current MVP foundation

What “visible” means in real field work

This layer makes the current investment concrete: intake, schedule, assignment, and mobile access create the shared foundation that future capabilities build from.

Where the vision matters

The best technology fades into the rhythm of the work.

The future of CardioConnect should not feel like another thing for reps and leaders to manage. It should be a connected layer that fits naturally into field work — helping teams get to the right procedure, with the right context, at the right time, so they can support physicians and reach more patients.

What connected work unlocks

Once the work is connected, the value extends beyond the calendar.

Scheduling becomes the front door to a broader field operating model — one that can improve readiness, reduce follow-up friction, and give leaders better signals.

04

Customer-facing intake

Let accounts submit structured procedure requests directly, reducing the need for field teams to collect and re-enter details.

05

Request completeness checks

Flag missing information at the point of intake so teams spend less time chasing down details later.

06

Automated case creation

Convert approved intake requests into draft cases or work items that can be reviewed, scheduled, and assigned.

07

Case readiness checks

Surface missing details, inventory gaps, documentation needs, or coverage risks before the rep arrives onsite.

08

Expiration-aware inventory use

Identify opportunities to use appropriate inventory before it expires and reduce avoidable waste.

09

Proactive replenishment signals

Use scheduled and completed procedures to predict when account or trunk stock may fall below par.

10

Smarter coverage planning

Recommend coverage based on skill fit, availability, proximity, workload, physician familiarity, and urgency.

11

Account demand signals

Turn procedure activity and coverage patterns into signals that help territory leaders prioritize account follow-up.

12

Regional capacity visibility

Help regional managers and AVPs see where demand, staffing, workload, and coverage pressure may be misaligned.

Implementation reality

Future exploration should honor the stack BSC already has.

This vision is not a blanket replacement story. It is a way to evaluate which layers should remain packaged, which should integrate more deeply, and where custom or AI-enabled experiences could create differentiated value.
